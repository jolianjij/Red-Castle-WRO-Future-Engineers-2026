01The mission

Two challenges on the same 3 × 3 m track, three laps each, fully autonomous.

In the Open Challenge the track is empty but the inner walls are repositioned every round, so the corridor width changes and no fixed driving line exists. In the Obstacle Challenge red and green traffic signs are added, and each dictates the side it must be passed on: green is passed on its left, red on its right. The car starts inside a magenta parking lot and must leave it before racing.

The direction of travel — clockwise or counter-clockwise — is not announced. The car has to work it out for itself, and getting it wrong ruins the whole run.

Our constraint is self-imposed: no distance sensors, no encoder, no IMU. One fixed-focus camera, and everything else is inference. The rules explicitly call their suggested component list "a suggestion rather than the requirements", so this is legal — but it means the car never actually knows how far anything is.

Learned the hard way

The Pi shut down every time the motor started. The cause was not current — it was that the Pi and the motor driver had no common ground. The only connection between them was the signal wire, so motor return current flowed back through the GPIO pin.

Separately, the motor must never be flipped from forward to reverse directly. It has to stop and settle first (STOP_FLIP_DELAY = 0.3 s), because the reverse voltage spike kills the regulator. Our motor() enforces this itself, so no calling code can get it wrong.

Distance is never measured — density is

The car has no way to measure distance, so it measures how much of the picture a wall fills. A nearer wall occupies more pixels. That single idea is the foundation of all the steering: 

left  = fraction of the LEFT half of the image that is wall
right = fraction of the RIGHT half
front = the same, straight ahead   → high means a corner

# measured on this car, this camera, this mounting:
density 0.1032 at 40 cm      slope 0.00501 per cm closer

Because that relationship depends on camera height, angle, lens and lighting, it must be re-measured whenever any of them change — which is why there is a tool for exactly that (§6).

What counts as a wall

Not simply "dark". A plain brightness test also catches the blue and orange corner lines and the mat's printed dot markings, all of which are dark too. The rule is two-case: 

wall = V < 32                        # very dark: definitely wall
    or (V < 62 and S < 90)           # dark AND colourless
then morphological open, k=5         # drops the printed dots
Measured · the decisive bug

For weeks the car drove into corners and nobody could find the control bug — because it was not a control bug. The wall detector was counting the blue and orange corner lines as walls, unevenly between the two image halves, injecting a phantom steering bias every frame.

on one failure frame, once lines were excluded: left 0.164 → 0.072 right 0.256 → 0.172

The two-case rule exists because the obvious single fix does not work: HSV saturation is numerically unstable at very low brightness, so a genuinely black wall can report S > 200 from sensor noise alone. A saturation-only test threw away 99 % of a real nose-to-wall frame.

Measured · three colours that were quietly wrong

Magenta was invisible. Its range stopped at H=170 while the block sits at H=171 — and red's wrap ran 170→5, so red was silently swallowing it. Magenta matched zero pixels while red claimed them.

Green was eating the red sign. With its floor at H=30 it matched 74 px on the red pillar — above the 55 px minimum area, so a red sign could have registered as green and been passed on the wrong side.

Orange was matching the mat, which is a warm cream, for 44 % of the band. Every one of these was found by checking each pair of colours for overlap, not by looking at each range on its own.

Measured · the parking wall was split between two colours

Running the parking exit on the real track exposed the worst of these. The wall’s body reads H 171–177, S 165–229, V 47–85. Red’s wrap began at 174, so the wall fell inside red — the largest “red” object in frame was a 119×20 slab of parking wall. Only the taller-than-wide test stopped it being treated as a traffic sign.

Magenta had been tuned on the block at a distance in good light (S 130, V 179). The same wall up close and shadowed is S 207, V 60, failing both bounds. It matched 290 px — a thin strip along the lit top edge of a wall filling most of the frame.

One surface spanning lit to shadow was being split between two colours. The boundary was chosen by trialling both values against the live frame, not by reasoning about it.

04How it decides

One pure function, one priority ladder, one branch per frame.

Both programs have the same three parts: a tunables block, a decide() function, and a loop. decide() touches no hardware and no camera — it is arithmetic on numbers. That is deliberate: it means the entire brain can be tested on a laptop with no robot attached, in under a second. 

button.wait_for_start()          # nothing moves until pressed
while True:
    view = R.look(cam)           # LOOK   one frame, all measured
    d    = decide(view, ...)     # THINK  one decision
    R.servo(d.steer)             # ACT
    R.motor(speed)
    if button.stop_pressed(): break

Obstacle Challenge ladder

Highest priority first. Exactly one rung answers each frame.

0ParkLeaving the lot. Must outrank the wall escape — inside the lot the magenta walls are close on purpose.
1KickA committed corner-exit turn. Outranks the escape because it is deliberately turning toward a wall — unless a wall closes from the opposite side, which cancels it.
2WallTouching a wall ends the run. Ramps 50 %→100 % and is floored by the normal controller.
3SignA red or green sign is in view — steer to place it on the correct side of the frame.
4HoldA sign was here in the last 3 s. Do not hand back to lane keeping mid-pass.
5LaneNothing else applies — outer-wall PD, the same law as the Open Challenge.

Open Challenge ladder

1EmergencyA wall is too close. Outranks everything.
2TurnScripted corner turn, fired by crossing the corner line — the line proves we are physically at the corner. A wall close ahead is the backup if a line is missed.
3LaneOuter-wall PD plus the drift trim.

05The six mechanisms

Each solves one problem the camera-only constraint creates.

1 · Follow the outer wall, not the middle

In the Open Challenge the inner walls move every round, so the corridor width changes. Centring chases a moving reference; the outer wall is the one thing that stays put. 

CW  →  error = left  − target      # outer wall is on the LEFT
CCW →  error = target − right      # outer wall is on the RIGHT
steer = 200·error + 55·Δerror      # deadband 0.02, clamp ±20°
Measured · why centring failed

A centring controller drove into every corner. Approaching one, both halves of the image darken together, so left − right ≈ 0 — the error vanishes exactly when the car most needs to turn. The outer-wall law has the opposite behaviour: the wall ahead raises the outer reading, which steers into the turn. Corners need no dedicated code at all.

2 · Which way round the track?

Orange means clockwise, blue counter-clockwise. But the two lines are not equally visible: orange is a warm colour on a warm mat and its saturation collapses with distance, while blue over-triggers on bluish background. Comparing raw pixel counts is therefore unfair, and it produced real misreads.

Measured · a CW run read as CCW raw pixels blue 0.030 > orange 0.025 → wrong answer own thresholds blue 0.86× < orange 2.08× → correct

Each colour now has its own threshold (orange 0.012, blue 0.035) and the decision compares confidence — how far each is over its own bar. If both look convincing at once the car waits for a clearer frame rather than locking a direction it can never undo.

3 · Passing a sign without knowing how far it is

We never estimate distance. We push the sign toward one side of the frame, and the car ends up on the correct side of it in the world: 

green → drive the sign toward x=180  # right of frame → we pass its left
red   → drive the sign toward x=140  # left of frame  → we pass its right

The target slides further outward as the sign gets nearer, so the car commits harder the closer it gets instead of clipping the corner of it. A sign must be taller than it is wide to count — that single test rejects the corner lines and stray mat pixels.

4 · Not abandoning a pass halfway

Detection flickers. Once a sign is lost, the lane keeper would immediately drag the car back across the pass it is halfway through. So for 3 seconds after the last sighting the car refuses to return to lane keeping — holding the sign's steering briefly, then running straight.

Measured · why the hold is in seconds one sign's contour area over four frames: 61 → 851 → 0 → 1060

Every debounce here is a wall-clock timer, never a frame count. The old 10-cycle guard was 0.33 s at 30 fps, and a flickering mask turned one physical line crossing into 36 counted crossings in a 45 s run.

5 · Getting out of the corner cleanly

The last sign before a corner leaves the car pushed to one side. If that push was toward the inner wall, the car enters the corner pointing across it and cannot see the next section at all — every closed-loop controller is then reacting to a wall it is about to leave behind. A short hard open-loop turn fixes the geometry:

DirectionCorner turnsInner wallSign that pushes you inwardKick
CWrightrightred30° right
CCWleftleftgreen30° left

The kick deliberately exceeds the 20° driving limit — that is its purpose — bounded by the linkage's real 35°. A wall closing from the opposite side cancels it.

6 · Leaving the parking lot decides the direction

The car starts inside the magenta lot. Whichever side is more blocked is the side it cannot leave by — so the free side is both the way out and the direction the lap will run. One measurement answers both questions, taken before the car has moved.

Camera seesWay outLap direction
more magenta on the leftleave rightCW (+1)
more magenta on the rightleave leftCCW (−1)

Because +1 already means "steer right" everywhere in the code, the exit steering is simply direction × angle. The measurement averages 8 frames while the car is stationary — the only moment in the run with no motion blur, and worth spending.

Corrected · why the black wall is excluded

A synthetic test first suggested magenta and the black wall were anti-correlated — the magenta wall occludes the black one behind it. On the real track they agree. Measured in the lot:

magenta L 0.716 R 0.449 -> left is blocked wall L 0.174 R 0.093 -> names the same side

The anti-correlation was an artifact of the synthetic image, where blank background counted as wall. The wall reading stays off for a plainer reason: magenta alone already gives a clear margin, and it is the direct signal — the lot is defined by magenta, whereas black is every wall on the track.

06Calibration

Three steps, and the order cannot be swapped.

Each step is measured through the previous one, so doing them out of order silently invalidates the earlier work. Every tool writes a file the code loads at start-up, so the competition code itself is never edited to retune. 

python tools/tune_colors.py            # 1. camera, then every colour
python tools/tune_walls.py --detector  # 2. what counts as a wall here
python tools/tune_walls.py             # 3. density ↔ centimetres

The colour tool is headless — a plain SSH terminal, no display. That is deliberate: at a venue the screen is the thing most likely not to work, and colour tuning is the one job that absolutely must happen when the light changes. It ends by checking every pair of colours for overlap, which is what catches a range that is correct alone but wrong beside its neighbour.

The trap

The wall detector does not use a colour range. It uses three brightness/saturation cuts. Tuning the "black" colour looks exactly like retuning the walls and changes nothing that drives the car — so the colour tool deliberately does not offer it, and --detector exists instead. If wall and mat overlap in brightness it refuses to write anything, because no threshold can separate them in that light.

07Operating it

Power the car. Press the button.

The button

One button on GPIO 19 does both jobs. Press once and the run starts — nothing moves before that. Press again and it stops, at any moment; this is the emergency stop, and the loop polls it every frame. Both edges are debounced, and a hold-off means releasing the start press cannot register as a stop.

Starting with no laptop

./autostart.sh on

The program then launches at every power-on. This is safe because it waits for the button — booting into it only arms the car. When a run finishes the service restarts it, re-arming for the next press. Which program runs is one line at the top of run.sh.

Wired networking

Venues ban wireless, so the Pi runs its own address server on Ethernet: plug a cable from a laptop and the laptop is handed an address automatically, with no configuration on its side. Wi-Fi and the cable coexist — with no cable the Ethernet route is marked linkdown and carries nothing. 

ssh pi@raspberrypi.local     # or 192.168.50.1
./tools/venue_net.sh wifi-off

wifi-off refuses unless the cable is physically connected. It checks the carrier rather than the address, because eth0 keeps its static address while unplugged — and that mistake would cut off the only route to the robot.

08Testing

Two commands, and neither needs the car to move.

python tools/test_logic.py    # 92 assertions, laptop only, no Pi
python tools/dryrun.py        # live camera, motor never touched

test_logic.py stubs the GPIO and camera modules so the real classes import on a laptop, then drives them with synthetic numbers. Every bug described in this document has a test pinning it, so it cannot come back quietly.

Why this exists

A build once shipped with a NameError on a live code path, because it had only been checked with py_compile. Compiling proves a file parses. It does not prove the code runs.

Every run also writes a CSV row per frame — timestamps, wall readings, line fractions, direction, quadrant, mode, steering, speed — which is how nearly every measurement quoted here was obtained. The Obstacle run additionally records the order of signs it passed and saves annotated frames of each pass.

The three things to do next

  1. Confirm the exit direction suits your track. The car reads the lot correctly and picks CW. Whether “blocked on the left means clockwise” matches your layout is a physical fact the code cannot check — if it leaves the wrong way, set PARK_INVERT = True.
  2. Time the corner kick. 0.9 s at 30° is an estimate; nobody has measured how fast this car rotates.
  3. Solve the laptop Ethernet, by BIOS or by a USB adapter. It is the single point of failure in the entire venue setup, and there is currently no spare.
